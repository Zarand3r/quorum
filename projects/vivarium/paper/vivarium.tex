{\large Molecular self-assembly in a transformer whose tokens are the molecules}}

\begin{abstract}
Molecular simulation requires a force field before it can simulate anything. Classical potentials are
written by hand; learned potentials regress a target computed by an independent theory. Both treat
physics as a specification the network consumes. We ask a different question: are the operations a
transformer already performs sufficient to express interaction physics, with the tokens
\emph{being} the molecules? We build vivarium, a molecular simulator in which every force is an
attention operation, every molecule is one token, and every timestep is one forward pass. A token's
channels are read as physical quantities, so charge and shape come from a single grounded contour
evaluated at the relative bearing to a neighbour. Under this restriction the hydrophobic effect
emerges from excluded volume, dispersion and electrostatics alone, with no per-species interaction
term. Water demixes from oil (order parameter $0.02 \to 0.51$), and three-bead amphiphiles bury
their tails from a disordered start (burial $0.64 \to 0.88$). A lipid bilayer does not form. We show the
failure is structural rather than kinetic: a hand-planted bilayer decays at every temperature tested
including zero. We report the representational fact that explains four falsified repairs, namely that
a contour encoding both size and charge cannot express a bulky neutral tail.
\end{abstract}

\section{Every simulator consumes a force field it did not derive}

Every molecular dynamics package begins with a potential. Lennard-Jones supplies dispersion, Coulomb
supplies charge, harmonic springs supply bonds, and a human chooses each functional form. Coarse-grained
models such as MARTINI go further and tune a per-species interaction matrix against experiment. The
physics arrives as a specification, and the simulator executes it.

Machine-learned interatomic potentials replaced the fitting with regression. SchNet, NequIP, MACE and
Allegro learn a potential energy surface from density-functional labels, and they reach chemical
accuracy on systems that classical force fields handle poorly. They inherit the original premise. The
network is a regressor for a potential that exists independently of it, and the network's own
operations are never asked to be the physics.

This leaves a question unasked. Are attention, position-wise feedforward layers and normalization
\emph{sufficient} to express interaction physics directly, with the tokens serving as the physical
entities rather than as an encoding of them? The question concerns expressiveness rather than
accuracy, and fitting cannot answer it. A model that regresses a potential says nothing about whether
attention can be one.

Forbidding hand-written potentials removes three standard tools at once. Divergent radial kernels
such as the $1/r^{12}$ hard core become unavailable, because a bounded attention weight cannot
diverge. Energy ledgers become unavailable, because bookkeeping a Hamiltonian is not an attention
operation. Global lattice sums become unavailable, so Ewald and particle-mesh Ewald cannot supply
long-range electrostatics. Every remaining force must decay smoothly and act locally.

We adopt that restriction and ask what physics survives it. Our target is lipid bilayer
self-assembly, chosen because it is the canonical emergent structure of soft matter, it has a sharp
success criterion, and coarse-grained models reproduce it with less machinery than we already have.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}\itemsep2pt
\item \textbf{Transformer-only molecular dynamics}, enforced mechanically. A fail-closed audit scans
every dynamical method for forbidden patterns, so it covers code added later by default and does not
rely on anyone remembering to register it.
\item \textbf{Grounded contour tokens.} A token's channels are read as position, charge distribution
and size. We evaluate the contour at the relative bearing to a neighbour, which turns electrostatics into a
relative-position attention head.
\item \textbf{Emergence without per-species force laws.} The hydrophobic effect and hydrophobic aggregation
appears from excluded volume, dispersion and electrostatics alone.
\item \textbf{A characterised negative result.} The lamellar phase is unstable under this force
field, and we identify the representational constraint that explains why four physics-derived
repairs failed.
\end{enumerate}

\section{A token is a molecule}

\subsection{Channels carry physical meaning}

A token is a vector whose channels we read as physical quantities:
\[
[\;\underbrace{\mathbf{p}}_{\text{position}}\;|\;\underbrace{C}_{\text{contour}}\;|\;\underbrace{r}_{\text{radius}}\;|\;\text{hidden}\;]
\]
The grounding matters. In a learned potential the latent is opaque and only the output is
interpretable. Here each force reads the channel it physically depends on, so a claim about the
physics is a claim about the representation and is falsifiable as such.

\subsection{The contour makes electrostatics an attention head}

The contour $C_i$ expands a molecule's charge distribution in real spherical harmonics. We read it at
the \emph{relative bearing} to a neighbour:
\[
\mathrm{nf}_i(j) \;=\; \langle C_i,\; Y(\hat{u}_{ij}) \rangle ,
\qquad \hat{u}_{ij} = \frac{\mathbf{p}_j-\mathbf{p}_i}{\lVert \mathbf{p}_j-\mathbf{p}_i \rVert}
\]
This is a RoPE-family relative-position term. It reports what face molecule $i$ turns toward $j$: a
contour with one positive lobe reads as a dipole, a uniform contour reads as apolar. The pair term
$\mathrm{prod}_{ij} = \mathrm{nf}_i(j)\,\mathrm{nf}_j(i)$ is positive for like faces and negative for
opposite faces, and it is symmetric under $i \leftrightarrow j$, which makes the resulting force
conservative.

Symmetry is not free. Row-stochastic softmax normalizes each row independently, so $A_{ij} \neq
A_{ji}$ and momentum is not conserved. Our conservative heads therefore drop the softmax denominator
and use a bounded kernel with a Gaussian radial factor,
\[
g_{ij} \;=\; \mathrm{gate}\!\left(\text{content}_i,\text{content}_j\right)\,e^{-\lambda d_{ij}^{2}},
\]
which places them in the linear/kernel-attention family. Section~\ref{sec:limits} states the cost of
that choice.

\subsection{Separating size from charge is what makes a tail expressible}
\label{sec:sep}

Our first contour encoded charge as a radial deviation, so a molecule's shape and its charge were the
same numbers. That representation forbids exactly one molecule: a lipid tail, which must be bulky and
neutral. A zero contour gave a neutral point with no surface to attract with, and a nonzero contour
gave extent together with charge. No parameter choice escapes this, and four separate repairs failed
before we located it.

Splitting size into its own channel resolves it. The radius channel carries dispersion strength and
the contour carries charge, so a molecule can now be bulky and neutral (a tail), bulky and charged (a
head), or small and strongly dipolar (water). We combine per-token well depths by the geometric rule
$\varepsilon_{ij} = \sqrt{\varepsilon_i \varepsilon_j}$, which guarantees a positive mixing energy
$\Delta E \propto (\sqrt{\varepsilon_{ii}}-\sqrt{\varepsilon_{jj}})^2$ and therefore supplies the
hydrophobic driving force by construction.

\subsection{Bonds are a fixed attention mask}

A three-bead lipid needs its beads held together. We implement a bond as a fixed pair mask carrying a
bounded symmetric kernel $\tanh\!\big((d-r_0)/w\big)$, which is masked attention rather than a
harmonic spring, and a third mask between the head and the far tail holds the chain extended. Token
count never changes; beads are existing tokens relabelled into molecules.

\subsection{Two invariants gate every number}

\textbf{Base-case identity.} With the polar features disabled the engine reproduces the original
engine bit for bit. Every feature added since remains auditable because this identity holds.

\textbf{Minimum image.} Periodic boundaries are valid only when every force is negligible at half the
box, otherwise a molecule interacts with its own periodic image. We report the worst kernel value at
$L/2$ and gate on it. The check caught a live violation in which the dispersion range exceeded the
box.

\section{The hydrophobic effect emerges; the bilayer does not}

\subsection{Water demixes from oil with no per-species term}

Water and a neutral solute separate under the three forces alone, with the order parameter rising
from $0.02$ to $0.51$. Nothing in the model names the pair. The separation follows from water's
electrostatic self-cohesion exceeding its dispersion attraction to a neutral surface, which is the
textbook energetic account of hydrophobicity.

\subsection{Amphiphiles bury their tails from a disordered start}

Three-bead lipids released from a disordered mixture aggregate with their tails inward. Tail burial
rises from $0.64$ to $0.88$, matching a hand-planted reference on every composition metric. Two tail
beads double the tail-tail contact area, so the aggregation drive scales with tail length, and head
repulsion acts at a different point from tail attraction. A single-bead amphiphile applies both at
the same point and never assembles.

\textbf{Takeaway.} Aggregation requires no per-species interaction matrix. Excluded volume,
dispersion and electrostatics on grounded tokens suffice for the first stage of the assembly pathway.

\subsection{The aggregates lack radial order, and the lamellar phase is unstable}

Assembly stops before the micelle. Tail burial alone cannot distinguish a micelle from an amorphous
aggregate, because burial rises for either. The distinguishing measurement is radial: a micelle places
its heads outside its tails. Measured from the aggregate's own centre of mass, the head-minus-tail
radius stays between $-0.11$ and $+0.22$ across a self-assembly run, so heads and tails occupy the
same shell. We report hydrophobic aggregation and withdraw any claim of micellar order. We measure order with orientation-agnostic parameters so that a
vesicle, a tilted sheet and a disc all register. Local nematic alignment separates lamellar order
from disorder. The fraction of antiparallel neighbours then separates a bilayer, whose two leaflets
meet tail to tail, from a micelle, whose lipids all point outward.

A hand-planted bilayer supplies the reference. It starts at nematic $+0.98$ and decays within a few
thousand steps under every condition we tested, including kT$=0$ and including three stiffness
regimes spanning a factor of ten. Zero temperature removes thermal melting and the timestep margin
removes numerical instability, so the lamellar phase is simply not stable under this force field.
The failure is structural rather than kinetic, which rules out longer runs and better initial
conditions as remedies.

\textbf{Takeaway.} Reporting only the assembly result would misdescribe the system. The planted
bilayer is the experiment that distinguishes "has not nucleated yet" from "cannot persist," and it
returns the second answer.

\subsection{Four physics-derived repairs, all falsified}

We derived each repair from the literature's parameter windows rather than by search, and each
failed. Widening the dispersion range to the value coarse-grained models require did not stabilise
the planted bilayer. Lowering the head charge to flip lateral cohesion positive did not either, even
though it reproduced the head-head to lateral ratio that single-site models prescribe. Correcting the
dispersion kernel to a valid mixing rule tripled the hydrophobic driving force and still left the
bilayer unstable. Making dispersion orientation-dependent routed charge back into van der Waals, and our own
faithfulness test rejected it, which is the outcome we want from that test.

\subsection{One bug invalidated an earlier benchmark history}

An electrostatic near-face read a token's far face rather than the face it presents, which made the
pair term asymmetric and the "conservative" force non-conservative by a measurable margin. Every
number recorded before the fix described a subtly non-physical system. We report this because the
system passed its tests throughout: the momentum-conservation test that caught it did not exist
until we wrote it.

\section{Related work}

\textbf{Learned interatomic potentials.} SchNet, NequIP, MACE and Allegro regress a potential energy
surface from first-principles labels, and equivariant message passing with spherical-harmonic features
is now standard. They target accuracy against a reference theory. We share their geometric machinery
and reject the framing: our question is whether attention can constitute a force law, which a
regression target cannot settle.

\textbf{Coarse-grained membrane models.} Cooke-Deserno lipids, MARTINI and single-site models such as
SiMPLISTIC assemble bilayers with less machinery than we have, which is what makes our negative result
informative rather than merely disappointing. Their parameter windows supply our reference values.
They also write their potentials down, including the engineered angular gates that make a single-site
amphiphile lamellar.

\textbf{Networks as physics.} Neural cellular automata and learned simulators share the instinct that
a network can be a dynamical law. They lack a grounded physical readout and a conservation
constraint, so their dynamics are not falsifiable as physics.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limits}

\textbf{No bilayer.} Aggregation holds; lamellar order does not.

\textbf{The thermostat is not attention.} We keep one exception, and it is load-bearing, since
nucleation is thermally activated and removing it makes the target unreachable in principle.

\textbf{The conservative heads are not canonical softmax attention.} They drop the normalizer to
achieve $F_{ij} = -F_{ji}$, which places them in the kernel-attention family. Calling them attention
heads is defensible and not free.

\textbf{System size.} A solvated spanning bilayer requires roughly a thousand tokens, and pair work
scales quadratically.

\section{Conclusion}

Attention over grounded tokens reaches further into soft-matter physics than we expected and stops
short of the target. The hydrophobic effect and amphiphile aggregation emerge from three forces with
no per-species term, which shows that a transformer's native operations express real interaction
physics rather than merely approximating a fitted surface. The lamellar phase does not follow, and
the planted-bilayer experiment locates the failure in the force field rather than in kinetics. The
representational constraint we identify, that a contour encoding both size and charge cannot express
a bulky neutral tail, generalises beyond this system: when a network's latent is read as physics, what
the representation can express bounds what can emerge.
