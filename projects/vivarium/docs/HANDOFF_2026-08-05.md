# Vivarium handoff — why does a bilayer never self-assemble?

**Written 2026-08-05 for an external reviewer.** Self-contained: no prior context assumed. The ask is
at the bottom, but read §6 and §7 first — they are where I think the error is, and I have not found it.

---

## 1. What the system is

A 2-D molecular/artificial-life simulator in which **every force is a transformer operation**. This is
a hard constraint, not an aesthetic:

- attention / MLP / LayerNorm / structured-linear only;
- **no divergent kernels** — no `1/d²`, no `1/r¹²`, nothing with a distance in a denominator. All
  kernels are bounded (Gaussian-like, `exp(−λd²)`, which decomposes into an inner product and is
  therefore literally an attention logit);
- **fixed token count** N — no creation or destruction;
- periodic box (torus), explicit solvent.

A "lipid" is a bonded chain of beads: one polar head + n tail beads. Water is an explicit polar
token. Interactions are Pauli exclusion (softmax-normalised repulsion), van der Waals (unnormalised
einsum attraction), and electrostatics (bearing-resolved, bounded).

The single non-attention operation is the Langevin thermal kick, which is acknowledged as such.

## 2. The goal, and the ladder

| rung | target | status |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | two lipids prefer tail-to-tail | **PASSES** |
| 1 | micelle (radial, heads out) | **ACHIEVED**, 2-D and 3-D, from a fully dispersed start |
| 2 | finite bilayer patch / ribbon | **PARTIAL** — elongated aggregates appear and persist |
| 3 | extended bilayer | **NEVER ACHIEVED** from self-assembly |
| 4 | vesicle (closed loop) | never reached |

## 3. The central contradiction

**The lamellar phase is stable when planted, and never nucleates from dispersion.**

- A *planted* 2-D bilayer at `repel=24` holds and *orders further*: splay 0.197 → 0.098, stable.
- A *planted* 2-D vesicle (closed loop, R=4, 50 lipids) is stable: local bilayer structure survives
  (splay 0.18–0.20, inside the bilayer band 0.00–0.21) while the loop's roundness degrades.
- A *dispersed* start at the same parameters produces micelles and stops.
- A **coexistence** run (planted strip + micelle in one box, `repel=24`): the strip persisted, the
  micelle stayed at 16 lipids, and **nothing transferred between them**. No lipid exchange.

So the target phase demonstrably exists and is mechanically stable. Disorder cannot reach it.

## 4. Every lever we have tested, and its verdict

All 2-D self-assembly, 63 lipids, dispersed start, seed 7 unless noted.

| lever | result | verdict |
|---|---|---|
| tail count (single → branched double) | aggregates 12.6 → 15.2, but `exposed` (unburied tail fraction) **worsens** 0.071 → 0.115 | overshoots packing parameter into the oil regime |
| `head_sigma` (steric head area) | largest aggregate 18 → **52**, `exposed` → **0.032** (best ever), but splay 0.253 → **0.695** | grows aggregates, destroys flatness |
| `head_q` (electrostatic head area) | splay 0.212 ± 0.023 across 3 seeds vs baseline 0.253 | marginal; at the band edge, not inside |
| force magnitude | no effect | scaling all forces is a rescaling of *time* |
| fixed temperature | kT 0.02 → 0.20 (**10×**): aggregates do not melt, packing holds 0.481, no extra fusion | no effect |
| annealing (hot → 0.02, 60k steps, vs a hot==cold control) | best case hot=0.25: splay 0.284 → 0.184, spanning 0.273 → 0.364 | real but far too small; nothing passes the gate |
| lipid margin (more lipids) | both metrics got *worse* | not the fix |

**Nothing has ever passed rung 3.** Every run fails on `spanning` — best 0.50 against a required 0.80.

### 4a. The size/flatness anti-correlation

The two requirements for a closed vesicle are anti-correlated under every lever found so far:

- closure needs N ≳ 31 lipids in one aggregate (from `R ≈ Na₀/4π` and a membrane half-thickness of 2.5);
- a bilayer needs splay < 0.21.

`head_sigma` ↑ gives size (18 → 52) and destroys flatness (0.253 → 0.695).
`head_q` ↓ gives flatness (0.253 → 0.212) and no size.
Combining them (`head_sigma=1.8` + `head_q=0.6`) **does not compose**: it keeps h1.8's size (52) *and*
h1.8's curvature (0.650). The size lever dominates.

## 5. Relevant geometry

- The single-tail plateau matches the **2-D disc cap** exactly: a disc micelle holds at most
  `n_max = 2πl/a₀`; with `l=2`, `a₀≈1` that is **12.6**, and the measured plateau is **12.2–12.6**.
- In strict 2-D the 3-D packing parameter is the wrong form. The correct one is `p_2D = 1 − l/(2R)`,
  so a filled circular micelle sits at ~1/2 and a **flat bilayer requires `p_2D → 1`**, i.e. R → ∞.
  There may be no molecular shape that prefers "flat" in 2-D — flatness is the zero-curvature limit.
- 2-D closure thermodynamics has no circular rim; an open strip has **two endpoints**, giving
  `ΔF_close = πκ₁/R − 2Γ_end` and `R_c,2D = πκ₁/(2Γ_end)`. This is *not* the 3-D
  `R_crit = (4κ_c + 2κ̄)/γ`.
- A prior sweep **measured** head spacing instead of assuming it: `head_sigma` 1.0 → spacing 1.999;
  1.8 → 2.604; 2.2 → 2.883. Head area scales **sub-linearly**, and effective `a₀` at `head_sigma=1.0`
  is already ~2.0. Recomputing `P` with measured areas, every *larger* head pushes *deeper* into the
  micelle regime.
- That same sweep varied `P` from **0.24 to 0.89 by two independent routes and the phase never
  changed.** The packing parameter, which is nature's mechanism for selecting lamellar over micellar,
  appears not to operate in this model.

## 6. Read this before trusting any number above

This project has found roughly **twenty measurement defects**, and in nearly every case the model was
healthier than the instruments claimed. Claims retracted after the instrument was fixed include:
"the micelles were collapses" (they were not), "3-D self-assembly is disordered" (it was micellar,
head-enrichment 3.00), "a planted bilayer is not a mechanical equilibrium" (the integrator was
exploding — velocity reached 11.5 at kT=0), and an `edge` metric that scored a rimless bilayer 0.727,
a closed loop 0.800, and a **random gas 0.727** — zero discriminating power, after an entire
vesicle-staging plan had been defined on it.

Defects found *this session alone*:

- a hollow/filled test that unwrapped about one bead and reported `R_core = 6.68` in an `L = 11` box —
  a circle of area 140 against a box of 121, so "encapsulated water" degenerated into *all the water*;
- a suspected percolation artifact in aggregate sizes that a cutoff sweep **refuted** (sizes stable
  from 1.6 down to contact-level 1.0), except at one condition where it was real;
- claims made from a single seed that replication killed ("flattest on record" was the low seed of three);
- an annealing "degradation" that the hot==cold control showed was just the 3× longer run.

**Consequence for you:** if a conclusion above seems to require an implausible physical mechanism,
the prior should be that the instrument is wrong, not the physics. Metrics that *are* calibrated
against planted references and null controls: `splay` (bilayer 0.00–0.21, micelle 0.47–0.63,
random null 0.60–0.70), `packing` (collapse floor 0.35), `exposed`, `head_enrichment` (max 3.0).

Two known-live caveats:

- **`splay` measures curvature, not order.** A tight micelle and a random gas both read ~0.7, so its
  "random null" collides with a genuine small-micelle value. Never read high splay as disorder alone.
- **`speed` is not a playback rate.** Measured: at matched physical time it scales thermal motion as
  **√speed** (RMS ratio 2.12 vs predicted √4 = 2.0). It is an effective-temperature dial, so any
  historical run that varied `speed` also varied kT without recording it.

## 7. The open contradictions I cannot resolve

1. **Stable but unreachable.** The planted lamellar phase is stable; dispersion never reaches it.
   That reads as a nucleation barrier — but a **10× temperature increase does not melt aggregates or
   drive any additional fusion**, which is what a purely kinetic trap should respond to. If it is
   kinetic, why is it insensitive to temperature? If it is thermodynamic, why is the planted phase
   stable?

2. **The log contains a genuine internal disagreement.** An earlier entry concludes the molecule is a
   *detergent* (P below the bilayer window) and that this is thermodynamic, "not a kinetic problem, a
   parameter problem or an annealing problem." A later entry proves the 2-D lamellar phase stable and
   reclassifies the whole thing as kinetics. Both cannot be fully right.

3. **`P` does not select the phase here**, though it is the mechanism that does so in nature. Either
   our `P` is not what we think it is (the measured-vs-assumed `a₀` discrepancy is suggestive), or
   the bounded/non-divergent kernels remove whatever makes `P` predictive.

4. **A force-scaling asymmetry we have measured but not judged.** Attraction is an *unnormalised*
   einsum (extensive — grows with coordination), while repulsion is *softmax-normalised* (capped;
   observed at 16% of its bound). Whether this breaks the balance that selects a lamellar phase is
   untested.

5. **An unexplained state.** At `repel=24` with annealing: packing 0.896 and the best `spanning` ever
   measured (0.500), yet `exposed` **0.524** — half the tail beads in solvent contact. Dense,
   extended, and hydrophobically naked simultaneously. That should not be stable.

## 8. Constraints on any proposed fix

- Must stay **transformer-only** (§1). A proposal requiring `1/d²`, variable token count, or an
  explicit energy ledger is out of scope, however correct physically.
- One flagged possible violation, currently unused: an optional `exp(−λ(d−r₀)²)` contact-shell term
  expands to a term linear in `d` (a norm), which is probably *not* expressible as attention.
  Default `r₀=0` takes the historical path, so nothing in this document depends on it.
- Iteration cost is real: ~52 ms/step, N=439, so a 60k-step run is ~52 minutes. `speed` cannot be
  raised for throughput — the stability product `speed·k_bond/(1−momentum) < 0.05` leaves only
  **1.17×** headroom, and exceeding it explodes the integrator (this has already happened once and was
  misread as the membrane melting). 78% of step time is in one non-core force routine, so the honest
  throughput lever is optimising that, not changing the dynamics.

## 9. The ask

**What are we doing wrong?**

Specifically:

1. Is the 2-D restriction itself the error? A 2-D "bilayer" is a strip whose flat state is the
   R → ∞ limit, so there may be no finite molecular geometry that prefers it. Is spontaneous lamellar
   self-assembly in strict 2-D actually *possible*, or have we set an unreachable target? (Note the
   reference model, Cooke–Deserno, given a finite aggregate in a large box also makes a **rod**, and
   only produces a bilayer when the periodic box is sized to force one.)
2. Given §7.1 — stable when planted, unreachable from disorder, insensitive to 10× temperature —
   what mechanism explains insensitivity to temperature in a system that is supposedly kinetically
   trapped?
3. Is the attraction/repulsion normalisation asymmetry (§7.4) sufficient to prevent a lamellar phase?
4. Is there a lever we have not touched at all? Everything in §4 is closed.

  0.05 inside a ring of z neighbours at fixed radius, force read as `vel` after one step from rest:

  Repulsion is NOT capped (grows x7.74 from z=1 to z=32) and for z >= 4 the ratio is FLAT at 0.051:

head-out INTERFACE. Any flatness claim must carry an interface metric alongside splay.

**#4 wrong success criterion: CONFIRMED, unfixed.** `stage3` still gates on `spanning > 0.80` although

**A NEW hypothesis, from the user, currently UNTESTED (two attempts, both confounded).**
Composition is 439 tokens = 189 lipid beads + 250 water: a 43.1% lipid token fraction, a **71.2% bead
area fraction** (random close packing for discs is ~82%), and **4.0 waters per lipid** where
coarse-grained membrane studies use 20-50. Steric jamming would explain the zero lipid exchange, the
insensitivity to 10x temperature, and the "non-ergodicity" the review identified.

Two tests were run and BOTH are confounded, so the hypothesis stands untested:
- diluting at fixed lipid count also drops lipid CONCENTRATION, and aggregation is diffusion-limited;
- raising hydration by removing lipids leaves too little material, and the metrics go out of range
  (packing 3.840, align nan).
The correct design holds lipid concentration fixed and scales box and water together (63 lipids, 800
water, bound ~15.5 gives hydration 12.7 at N=989, roughly 5x step cost).

**`speed` (affects how to read the whole log).** Fixed 2026-08-06 by rescaling the Langevin kick
against a per-engine construction reference, so byte-identity holds at every configured speed and only
slider moves are corrected. Two engines BUILT at different speeds are still not equivalent -- a
documented limitation, since a global reference would rescale one engine family's noise.

**Iteration cost is unchanged.** The speed fix buys no throughput: the stability product
speed*k_bond/(1-momentum) < 0.05 sits at 0.0429 (1.17x headroom) and limits the DETERMINISTIC step.
Profiling: 78% of step time in `polar_pack._extra_force`, 31% in `_near_face` alone (cos/sin of
k*ang over an (N,N) float64 array). Chebyshev recurrence + float32 is the honest 2-3x.

**Revised ask for the next reviewer.** The force-law hypothesis is largely closed and attraction range
is not the answer. The live candidates are now: (a) steric jamming / hydration, properly tested;
(b) missing nematic-alignment physics (review §9.3), still unimplemented; (c) the wrong success gate.
