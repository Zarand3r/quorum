# Vivarium: why a 2-D vesicle does not self-assemble

**Written 2026-08-09 for an external reviewer. Supersedes HANDOFF_2026-08-05.md**, which framed the
problem before the key quantities were measured. Self-contained; no prior context assumed.

The single sentence version: **a vesicle needs 81 lipids in one aggregate (measured, in a solvent that
is itself broken), self-assembly caps at 45, and whether that cap is kinetic or an equilibrium
self-limited size is UNRESOLVED.**

Read §5.3 and §5.4 before anything else: at the operating point where every good structural result was
obtained, the solvent has condensed into droplets and 94% of the box is vacuum. Those results may be
surface-tension artifacts. The 81-lipid threshold was also measured in that regime and must be
remeasured once the solvent is repaired -- it is not a universal geometric constant.

---

## 1. The system and its hard constraints

A 2-D molecular simulator in which **every deterministic force is a transformer operation**:

- attention / MLP / LayerNorm / structured-linear only;
- **no divergent kernels** — no `1/d²`, no `1/r¹²`. All kernels are bounded (Gaussian-like
  `exp(−λd²)`, which decomposes into an inner product and is literally an attention logit);
- fixed token count N; periodic box; explicit solvent;
- the Langevin thermal kick is the one acknowledged non-attention operation.

A lipid is a bonded chain: one polar head + n tail beads. Water is an explicit polar token. The live
chemistry is a species-pair matrix `eps_pair`: water-water 0.60, water-head 0.60, water-tail 0.02,
head-head 0.10, head-tail 0.05, tail-tail 1.00.

**Excluded volume is a SYMMETRIC UNNORMALISED PAIR SUM, not softmax attention.** An earlier version of
this document said "softmax-normalised repulsion", which was wrong and misled two reviewers into
ranking it the primary suspect. `bicelle2d` sets `repel_contact = 1.0`, which selects:

    overlap = clip(contact_distance - dist, 0, None)   # per pair, bounded, zero beyond contact
    repel   = einsum("ij,ijc->ic", drive, dirn)        # UNNORMALISED sum over all j

so `F_i = sum_j f_R(r_ij) * r_hat_ij` with each pair term bounded and `F_ij = -F_ji`. Repulsion is
therefore EXTENSIVE in coordination, which is what excluded volume requires. A softmax path exists in
`pack.py` but is the `else` branch and is not taken by this engine.

Measured, one bead displaced inside a ring of z neighbours at fixed radius (2026-08-05):

    z     repel_only   attract_only   ratio A/R
    0     0.00000e+00  1.71442e-12    (control passes)
    1     5.99335e-01  7.61649e-01    1.271
    4     5.99335e-01  2.94743e-02    0.051
    8     1.16550e+00  5.92824e-02    0.051
    16    2.32194e+00  1.18565e-01    0.051
    32    4.64010e+00  2.37130e-01    0.051

Repulsion grows x7.74 from z=1 to z=32 -- NOT capped -- and for z >= 4 attraction and repulsion scale
in LOCKSTEP. There is no coordination-dependent weakening of repulsion.

**Reciprocity is tested and passes**: total internal momentum stays below 1e-9 per token over 200
steps at kT=0 in both engines (`test_no_net_drift_newtons_third_law`). **Conservativity is NOT
tested** -- reciprocity does not imply integrability, and a many-body force can be reciprocal without
deriving from a potential. That remains genuinely open.

**This constraint has a measured physical consequence** (§5.3): a bounded repulsion has a *finite*
maximum force, so under pressure beads can pass through each other where a real `1/r¹²` core cannot
be crossed. That is not a stylistic limitation; it changes what phases are reachable.

## 2. What is now measured (the reason this document supersedes the last one)

### 2.1 The vesicle minimum is 81 lipids

Planted closed loops, 3000 steps, `encloses` = enclosed solvent (planted-loop band 18–26, flat sheet
~10, droplet 1, random 0):

| R | lipids | encloses t=0 → t=3000 | bilayer_frac | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3.0 | 37 | 1 → 6 | 0.345 | opens |
| 5.0 | 63 | 2 → 7 | 0.487 | opens |
| **6.5** | **81** | 9 → **17** | **0.714** | **lumen holds and grows** |

**A vesicle is stable in this model once formed.** The previous threshold used throughout the project
("N ≥ 31") came from *assuming* `R ≈ Na₀/4π` with a half-thickness of 2.5. It is low by a factor of
2.6, which is why 37-lipid aggregates kept looking as though they should close.

### 2.2 Spontaneous curvature is zero

Planted arcs, 1500 steps:

| R planted | R after | drift |
|---|---|---|
| 3.0 | 3.31 | +0.22 (flattens) |
| 4.0 | 4.31 | +0.28 (flattens) |
| 5.5 | 5.56 | +0.04 (holds) |
| 7.0 | 7.01 | −0.01 (holds) |
| 9.0 | 9.02 | +0.02 (holds) |

Nothing above R≈5 drifts either way. **The membrane never wants to curve; it only tolerates
curvature above R≈5 and actively unbends below it.** So closure cannot be driven by preferred
curvature — the aggregate must first be large enough to bend at all.

Note the open-arc result *over-predicted* closure: extrapolating it gave 63 lipids, and the closed
loop at 63 opens. A closed loop compresses its inner leaflet in a way an open arc cannot show.

### 2.3 The packing parameter does select the phase

Sweeping head area (which sets `a₀`, hence `P = v/(a₀l)`), measured with `bilayer_frac`:

| head_sigma | tails | bilayer_frac |
|---|---|---|
| 0.7 | single | 0.429 |
| **1.0** | single | **0.746** |
| 1.4 | single | 0.127 |
| 1.0 | double | 0.349 |
| 1.8 | double | 0.048 |

A sharp interior optimum, confirmed visually on both sides (head_sigma 1.0 shows head-tail-tail-head
ribbons; 1.4 shows radial micelles). This **overturns** an earlier conclusion that "the packing
parameter does not operate in this model", which was reached with metrics since retracted.

It also explains a long-standing frustration: **head_sigma 1.0 is the default**, so every sweep was
walking away from an optimum nobody knew they were standing on, and lever after lever read as "no
effect".

### 2.4 Two tails do not help *in 2-D*, and the geometry says why

Measured molecular widths (not assumed — the assumption was already wrong once here):

| molecule | head width | tail width | ratio | shape |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| single tail | 1.00 | 1.14 | 0.87 | rectangle → flat |
| branched double | 1.00 | 1.91 | 0.52 | wedge |

Matching the head to the measured 1.91 tail width still gives micelles (0.111 at 1.9, 0.143 at 2.0).
**Width-matching is necessary but not sufficient; the branched topology itself micellizes in 2-D.**

The likely reason, offered as a hypothesis: in 3-D a single tail is a *cone* because head **area**
scales as r² against a fixed tail cross-section — which is exactly why nature requires two tails. In
strict 2-D a linear chain is already a rectangle, so the requirement inverts. **If that is right, this
project has been testing correct physics in the one geometry where it does not apply.** A reviewer
should attack this claim first; it is load-bearing and only partly evidenced.

## 3. The gap: 45 emergent against 81 required

Best emergent structures (clump start supplies the material; dispersed starts cap at 18–20):

| condition | largest aggregate | bilayer_frac | packing |
|---|---|---|---|
| dispersed, dry optimum | 18 | **0.746** | 0.502 |
| clump, 100 lipids | 37 | 0.640 | 0.373 |
| clump, 120 lipids, hydrophobic 2.0 | **45** | 0.525 | 0.357 |

**Whether the cap is kinetic or thermodynamic is UNRESOLVED, and an earlier claim here that it was
kinetic was wrong.** That claim rested on "`N·μ + 2Γ_end` is linear in N, so nothing caps it", which
omits the translational/mixing entropy of having M separate aggregates:

    F(N_tot, M) ~ N_tot*mu + M*E_end + kT*M*[ln(M/V) - 1]

End-cap energy favours fewer aggregates, entropy favours more, and minimising the sum gives a FINITE
equilibrium aggregation number -- the standard living/wormlike-micelle result. So a self-limited
equilibrium size is fully consistent with everything observed.

Suggestive existing data: at matched concentration, 63 lipids gives ~5 cores of ~12 and 100 lipids
gives ~11 cores of ~9. Mean size roughly constant while M scales with N_tot is the signature of
equilibrium self-limitation, not arrest.

Zero lipid transfer in a coexistence run proves the dynamics are non-ergodic on the observation time.
It does NOT prove a larger aggregate is thermodynamically favoured.

## 4. A structural change that was implemented and refuted

Nothing in the force law rewarded lipid **alignment**, so an explicit nematic term was added
(default 0.0, byte-identical when off):

    w_ij = eps_ij · exp(−λd²) · (1 + nematic·(u_i·u_j)²)

Transformer-only: the squared inner product is an inner product of outer-product features. Result:
**worse everywhere** — 0.746 → 0.540 dry, 0.175 → 0.111 submerged. The reason is precise: a squared
dot rewards *parallel* lipids wherever they sit, which is a nematic droplet, not a layered membrane.
**Rewarding alignment produces alignment, not layering.**

## 5. Read this before trusting any number above

This project has found **28 measurement defects**. In most cases the model was healthier than the
instruments claimed; in several the reverse. If a conclusion here seems to require an implausible
mechanism, the prior should be that the instrument is wrong.

### 5.1 Metrics that are calibrated and trustworthy

| metric | calibration |
|---|---|
| `bilayer_frac` | planted ribbon **0.984**, planted micelle **0.000**, holding from 20 to 150 lipids. **2-D only** — returns nan in 3-D (§5.4) |
| `splay` | bilayer 0.00–0.21, micelle 0.47–0.63, random null 0.60–0.70. Valid in 3-D (planted bilayer 0.000, dispersed 0.852) |
| `packing` | collapse floor 0.35. **One-sided** — unbounded above, rises with dilution, never compare across densities |
| `encloses` | planted loop 18–26, flat sheet ~10, droplet 1, random 0. Reads high on percolating networks |
| `solvent_packing`, `wet_frac` | 1.0 = solvent at contact / fully submerged |

`bilayer_frac` works by **conjunction** — *paired* (reversed axes, heads split across a shared tail
core) excludes the nematic droplet; *locally flat* excludes the micelle. Neither half works alone, and
both failures were measured: a leaflet-pair test alone scores planted **micelle 1.000** against ribbon
0.984, because in 2-D a small micelle *is* locally two leaflets meeting at a core.

### 5.2 Metrics retired

`edge` (rimless bilayer 0.727, closed loop 0.800, random gas 0.727 — no discriminating power);
`lamellar` and `aspect` (measure the box); `opposed` (0.083 window, one state scored *above* the
planted bilayer); `exposed` for cross-run use (confounded by solvent density).

### 5.3 The solvent collapses at the standing operating point (defect #27)

| condition | steps | median water nn / contact | inside contact | bound dimers |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| repel 12 | 4k | 0.76 | 62% | 26% |
| **repel 12** | **20k** | **0.43** | **100%** | **40%** |
| repel 24 | 20k | 0.89 | 5% | 5% |
| repel 48 | 20k | 0.96 | 0% | 0% |

At the operating point the solvent progressively collapses into bound dimers — two beads sharing one
well. **This is the bounded-kernel constraint biting** (§1). It is time-dependent, which is why a short
relaxation looks fine and it went unnoticed project-wide.

**Consequence:** the best bilayer results (0.746) were collected in a collapsing solvent. Those ribbons
may form partly because water condenses out and leaves the lipids in effective vacuum. **Whether they
survive in a real solvent is untested** and is the single most valuable control anyone could run.

This also collapses the long-standing "order vs hydration" wall into one problem: **order has only ever
been observed while the solvent was broken.**

### 5.4 Other defects a reviewer should know

- **3-D is unvalidated.** `bilayer_frac` has no power there (planted 3-D bilayer scores 0.000, same as
  a gas). Worse, the 3-D box is **too small to hold its own membrane**: thickness 5.0 against L/2 = 4.0,
  so minimum-image wraps cross-leaflet separations. An earlier conclusion that "the 3-D lamellar phase
  is not stable" came from that box and is unsupported.
- **Five dead sliders** shipped in the hosted UI (`k_tail`, `k_hydro`, `repel_contact`, `sink_repel`,
  `rigidity`) — controls that render, move, and change nothing. Found by a liveness test that perturbs
  each knob and requires the trajectory to diverge.
- **`speed` was an effective-temperature dial**, not a playback rate (measured RMS ratio 2.12 vs a
  predicted √4 = 2.0). Fixed against a per-engine reference; historical runs are byte-identical.

## 6. The ask

The remaining problem is **one number: 45 → 81 lipids in a single aggregate.** Size, order, curvature,
the target itself, and the solvent are all now measured. Six independent levers have failed to move
coalescence, and it is kinetic — aggregates neither exchange lipids nor fuse.

Specifically:

1. **What makes 2-D bilayer domains merge rather than freeze at grain boundaries?** This is the whole
   problem. Standard answers (temperature, annealing, longer runs, more material, stronger cohesion)
   are all tested and listed in §3.
2. **Does the order survive a non-collapsing solvent?** (§5.3.) If not, the best results in this
   project are artifacts and the target changes.
3. **Is the 2-D restriction itself the error?** (§2.4.) If the two-tail requirement genuinely inverts
   in 2-D, the honest move may be 3-D — but 3-D needs a working metric and a bigger box first (§5.4),
   at roughly 330 lipids, N≈1500, ~2 h per run.
4. **Is the bounded-kernel constraint (§1, §5.3) compatible with a real liquid at all?** A finite
   maximum repulsion means there is always a pressure at which beads interpenetrate. Raising `repel`
   defers it but also destroys lipid order.

the entries they void). Metric definitions and their null controls are in `harness.py` and
`tests/test_metric_truth.py`. Tools: `bench_step.py`, `render_state.py`.
