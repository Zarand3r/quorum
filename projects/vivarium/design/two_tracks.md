# Two tracks: the molecular (energy) track and the organism (predictive) track

> Reasoning only — **no code, no decision to build yet.** This records a second, parallel
> formulation of vivarium ("do what molecules do") alongside the predictive one we've been
> designing, and the through-line that makes building *both* well-motivated rather than
> redundant.

The two tracks are **the same single transformer wired two different ways**, and they are
**the two halves of `ẋ = −D∇Φ + J`** (see [`potential_flux.md`](potential_flux.md))
instantiated at **two biological scales**. Building both turns the central open question —
*does a single scalar/energy suffice for life, or do you need the learned/predictive
structure?* — from an ablation into a first-class comparison of two watchable systems on one
aliveness harness.

---

## 1. The one distinction that generates everything else

**How is the transformer used?**

- **Organism / predictive track** — the transformer is a **policy / update rule**. Its
  *forward pass decides* how agents move; **backprop trains it** every tick. The agent is a
  **cell/organism**, and its objective is **predictive surprise** about its local world
  (variational free energy). This is the track [`../SPEC.md`](../SPEC.md),
  [`related_work.md`](related_work.md) §3, and [`../DECISIONS.md`](../DECISIONS.md) describe.

- **Molecular / energy track** — the transformer instead **defines a scalar energy**
  `E_θ(X)` over the whole token configuration, and the molecules **roll downhill on it**:
  ```
  Ẋ = −∇_X E_θ(X)  +  J
  ```
  No policy, no decisions — the state dynamics *are* the gradient of an energy, exactly what
  molecules do (binding, folding, self-assembly = descending a free-energy landscape), with
  `J` the metabolic drive keeping it off-equilibrium. The agent is a **molecule**.

**This is a principled wiring, not a metaphor.** A transformer's attention *is* the gradient
of an energy — modern Hopfield networks (Ramsauer et al. 2020, "Hopfield Networks is All You
Need") show attention = one step of gradient descent on an associative-memory energy. So "the
transformer is a molecular force field" is real. It is still **one transformer**: you read out
`∇_X E` (a backward pass *over the inputs*) to get forces, instead of using the forward pass as
a policy.

## 2. What "do what molecules do" forces you to give up (the honest cost)

**Molecules don't learn.** A water molecule never updates its parameters. So the molecular
track has a fundamentally different clock structure, and "the simulation *is* the training"
(the north star) **changes meaning.** Two honest sub-options:

- **M-track (1) — pure physics, no training.** Fix `θ` (hand-set or random "chemistry"), run
  `−∇E_θ + J`, and *watch* self-assembly / Turing patterns / dissipative structures emerge.
  Philosophically clean (molecules have no goals, no objective function); aliveness is measured
  on a forward-only sim. Cost: there is no "transformer being trained" — the transformer is
  *used as physics*. **Likely the sane first cut** — it also sidesteps energy-model training
  difficulty (contrastive divergence / sampling / stability).
- **M-track (2) — slow evolutionary clock.** `θ` = the chemistry, *evolved* over a slow outer
  loop so the soup self-maintains / persists. This is the **two-clock** design we rejected for
  the organism track (D8) — but here it is **biologically correct**, because chemistry *is*
  fixed on the molecular timescale and changes only on the evolutionary one. The objection that
  killed two-clock for "learn while living" **inverts** and becomes the right answer for
  "molecules + evolution."

**The objective problem flips too.** The predictive track has an obvious self-supervised
objective (surprise). The molecular track has **no natural per-agent objective** — molecules
don't optimize anything. So either *no objective* (M-1), or the only honest objective is
**survival / persistence at the population level** (M-2) — which is *fitness*, the
population-scalar from [`potential_flux.md`](potential_flux.md) §2, and exactly what the
economy substrate did with ES-over-survival. That lands the molecular track in
**origin-of-life / autocatalytic-set** territory: *which fixed-point assemblies persist against
the drive.*

## 4. Why both — the through-line

The two tracks are **literally the `−∇Φ` half and the predictive half of
`ẋ = −D∇Φ + J`, at two biological scales.** So building both is the paper's central open
question made concrete and watchable:

- Does the **energy-descent (molecular)** formulation, driven off-equilibrium, produce measured
  aliveness?
- Does the **predictive (organism)** formulation?
- Do they land in the **same regime** on the same ungameable harness — and if so, is that the
  duality `potential_flux.md` predicts, or do they diverge (revealing that the learned/predictive
  structure buys something the pure energy cannot)?

This promotes the E-flux4 "gradient-only control vs predictive" ablation
([`potential_flux.md`](potential_flux.md) §5) from a knob inside one sim to a **first-class
pair of sims** — a stronger, more honest experiment.

## 5. Honest cautions (carry these into any build decision)

1. **The molecular track overlaps with fold/morph — the differentiator is a *dynamic
   environment* (plus population).** Docking = energy descent = the molecular regime; **morph is
   already a proto-molecular sim** (reaction-diffusion energy + skew-term flux). The sharp
   distinction:
   - **Morph:** the dynamics are *fixed* and non-convergence comes from an **internal** rotational
     (skew) term — the landscape/rule doesn't change; an internal stir keeps it moving. Closed
     system, no environment, single growing field from a seed.
   - **Molecular vivarium:** the energy landscape itself is **environment-conditioned**,
     `E_θ(X; s(t))`, where `s(t)` is a slowly **drifting external field** — so the *minima
     themselves drift* and the molecules must **continually re-assemble to track them.** Plus it
     is a **population** of many discrete interacting molecules (birth/death/competition
     possible), not one seed-grown field.

   So the drive `J` is **external and dynamic** (a moving landscape) rather than **internal and
   static** (morph's skew). This is more life-like — the environment changes and the assembly
   must keep up — and it echoes the **economy** sim's drifting resource source. Lineage:
   molecular vivarium ≈ *economy's dynamic environment* + *morph/fold's energy substrate* +
   *population*. Caveat: a dynamic environment **alone** would just be "morph with a drifting
   input"; the population + explicit energy (`−∇_X E_θ`) formulation + measured-aliveness harness
   are what make it a distinct sim, not the drift by itself. Keep the code separate regardless
   (D1); this is a conceptual-overlap warning, not a merge.
2. **The molecular track weakens the north star.** "Watching it run = watching it learn" is the
   organism track's signature. The molecular track is either *pure physics* (no learning to
   watch) or *slow-clock evolution* (learning is off-screen). Both are legitimate — accept the
   trade deliberately, don't discover it later.
3. **Energy-model *training* is hard — so avoid training the energy at first.** M-1 (fixed /
   evolved `θ`, no per-tick energy learning) sidesteps contrastive-divergence / sampling /
   stability problems. Reach for a trained energy only with cause.
4. **Same `J` question in both.** The drive is either an external drift or an internal
   non-gradient (skew / rotational) term — the morph skew term is exactly this. Whether a purely
   internal `J` suffices (no environment) is open in both tracks (see `potential_flux.md`
   E-flux5 and `global_vs_local.md` Q7).
